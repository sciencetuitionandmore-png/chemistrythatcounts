Rate of reaction: solids and acid

Core practical 3.15

Aim: Investigate the effect of changing the surface area of a solid and the concentration of hydrochloric acid on the rate of reaction, using marble chips, magnesium or zinc.

Safety
  • Wear eye protection. Dilute hydrochloric acid is an irritant to eyes and skin.
  • Wash off any acid spills on skin with plenty of water.
  • Make sure the delivery tube is not blocked, so pressure cannot build up in the flask.
  • Hydrogen is flammable and magnesium powder can catch fire. Keep flames away, except a lighted splint to test a small tube of gas.

Method

  1. Choose a run (which experiment, which variable).
  2. Measure 10 cm³ of hydrochloric acid into the conical flask.
  3. Weigh 2.00 g of marble chips.
  4. Fill the measuring cylinder with water and turn it upside down in the trough.
  5. Add the solid to the acid, put the bung in quickly and start the stopwatch.
  6. Read the volume of gas every 30 s for 5 minutes.
