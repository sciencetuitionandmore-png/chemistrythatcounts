Finding the activation energy of a reaction

IAL Chemistry · Core Practical 10 · Unit 6

Aim: to measure how the time for the bromide–bromate(V) reaction changes with temperature, and use an Arrhenius plot to find the activation energy, Ea.

Safety
  • Wear eye protection. Phenol is toxic and corrosive, even as a dilute solution: avoid skin contact and wash off any splashes at once.
  • Bromine is produced in the tube. It is toxic and corrosive: work in a well-ventilated room and do not smell the mixture.
  • Sulfuric acid (0.5 mol dm−3) is an irritant. Potassium bromate(V) is toxic and an oxidiser.
  • Water at 50–60 °C can scald. Use a thermostatic water bath or take care with a kettle.

Part 1Do the experiment

BrO3−(aq) + 5Br−(aq) + 6H+(aq) → 3Br2(aq) + 3H2O(l)

Bromine first reacts with the phenol. When all the phenol is used up, free bromine bleaches the methyl red. So the red colour disappears after a fixed amount of bromine has formed.

    Part 2Record your results

    Copy your readings. Use T / K = T / °C + 273. Give 1/T to 3 significant figures (×10−3) and ln(1/t) to 2 decimal places.

    Arrhenius equation:  ln k = −Ea/RT + ln A   and 1/t ∝ k, so ln(1/t) = −(Ea/R)(1/T) + constant
    RunT / °CT / K1/T / 10−3 K−1t / sln(1/t)
    Drag the red circles to draw your line of best fit (or focus the graph: arrow keys move, 1/2 picks a circle).
    Gradient of your line: – K
    1. Gradient of your line = K (note the 10−3 on the x-axis: e.g. −6600)
    2. Gradient = −Ea/R, so Ea = −gradient × 8.31 ÷ 1000 = kJ mol−1
