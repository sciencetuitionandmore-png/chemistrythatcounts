Redox titrations: iron(II) with manganate(VII), and iodine with thiosulfate

IAL Chemistry · Core Practical 13 · Unit 6

Aim: To use redox titrations to find the value of x in FeSO4·xH2O and the percentage by mass of copper in copper(II) sulfate crystals.

Safety
  • Wear eye protection throughout. Dilute sulfuric acid (1 mol dm−3) irritates eyes and skin.
  • Copper(II) sulfate is harmful if swallowed, causes serious eye irritation and is toxic to aquatic life: put residues in the waste bottle. Iron(II) sulfate is harmful if swallowed.
  • Always use a pipette filler. Never pipette by mouth.
  • Potassium manganate(VII) and iodine stain skin and clothes. Wipe up spills at once. Fill the burette below eye level and remove the funnel before titrating.

A. Iron(II) with manganate(VII): finding x in FeSO4·xH2O

MnO4−(aq) + 8H+(aq) + 5Fe2+(aq) → Mn2+(aq) + 5Fe3+(aq) + 4H2O(l) · M(FeSO4) = 151.9 g mol−1 · M(H2O) = 18.0 g mol−1

B. Iodine with thiosulfate: percentage of copper in CuSO4·5H2O

2Cu2+(aq) + 4I−(aq) → 2CuI(s) + I2(aq) · I2(aq) + 2S2O32−(aq) → 2I−(aq) + S4O62−(aq) · Ar(Cu) = 63.5

Part 4Exam practice

Type numbers only. Written answers: write first, then open the mark scheme and tick the points you made.

1 5.60 g of a partly dehydrated sample of FeSO4·xH2O was dissolved in dilute sulfuric acid and made up to 250.0 cm3. 25.0 cm3 portions were titrated with 0.0200 mol dm−3 KMnO4. The mean titre was 25.00 cm3. Calculate the value of x, to the nearest whole number. (3)

n(MnO4−) = 0.0200 × 25.00/1000 = 5.00 × 10−4 mol (1)
n(Fe2+) in 250 cm3 = 5.00 × 10−4 × 5 × 10 = 0.0250 mol; M = 5.60 ÷ 0.0250 = 224 g mol−1 (1)
x = (224 − 151.9) ÷ 18.0 = 4.0, so x = 4 (1)

2 6.50 g of impure copper(II) sulfate crystals, CuSO4·5H2O (M = 249.7 g mol−1), was dissolved and made up to 250.0 cm3. A 25.0 cm3 portion was added to excess KI(aq). The iodine released needed 24.80 cm3 of 0.100 mol dm−3 Na2S2O3. Calculate the percentage purity of the crystals, to 3 significant figures. (3)

n(S2O32−) = 0.100 × 24.80/1000 = 2.48 × 10−3 mol = n(Cu2+) in 25.0 cm3 (1)
In 250 cm3: 0.0248 mol; mass = 0.0248 × 249.7 = 6.19 g (1)
Purity = 6.19 ÷ 6.50 × 100 = 95.3% (1)

3 Why is hydrochloric acid not used to acidify the iron(II) solution before titration with KMnO4? (1)

C. E⦵(MnO4−/Mn2+) = +1.51 V is more positive than E⦵(Cl2/Cl−) = +1.36 V, so MnO4− oxidises Cl− to Cl2. Extra KMnO4 is used up.

4 0.0100 mol of Cu2+ ions reacts with excess iodide ions. What amount of thiosulfate ions reacts with the iodine released? (1)

B. 2Cu2+ → 1 I2 → 2S2O32−, so Cu2+ : S2O32− = 1 : 1.

5 Write the half-equations for the oxidation of iron(II) ions and for the reduction of manganate(VII) ions in acid solution. Use them to construct the overall ionic equation. (3)

Allow equilibrium signs in half-equations. Ignore state symbols. Do not award the third mark if electrons remain.

6 (a) A burette has an uncertainty of ±0.05 cm3 for each reading. A titre was 12.50 cm3. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the titre. (1)

Two readings: 2 × 0.05 = 0.10 cm3. 0.10 ÷ 12.50 × 100 = 0.80%

6 (b) Suggest how the percentage uncertainty in the titre could be reduced, using the same burette. (1)

Do not award "repeat the titration" or "use a more accurate burette".
