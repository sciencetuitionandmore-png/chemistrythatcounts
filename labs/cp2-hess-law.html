Finding an enthalpy change using Hess's Law

IAL Chemistry · Core Practical 2 · Unit 3

Aim: To find the enthalpy change for the thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate, CaCO3(s) → CaO(s) + CO2(g), from two reactions with hydrochloric acid that can be measured.

Safety
  • Wear eye protection. 2.0 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid is an irritant to the eyes and skin.
  • Calcium oxide is corrosive: it reacts with water in the eyes or on damp skin and gets hot. Do not touch it; use a spatula and avoid dust.
  • The reaction of calcium oxide with acid is very exothermic. Add the solid steadily and keep your face away from the cup.
  • Clamp the burette at eye level or below and fill it below eye level, using a funnel.
Key fact: Hess's Law says the enthalpy change for a reaction is the same whatever route is taken, if the start and end conditions are the same.
