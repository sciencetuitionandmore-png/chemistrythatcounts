Investigating some electrochemical cells

IAL Chemistry · Core Practical 12 · Unit 6

Aim: To measure the e.m.f. of cells made from two half-cells and compare it with the value calculated from standard electrode potentials.

Safety
  • Wear eye protection. Silver nitrate solution (1.0 mol dm−3) can cause serious eye damage and stains skin black: wear gloves.
  • Copper(II) sulfate solution (1.0 mol dm−3) is harmful if swallowed and irritates eyes and skin.
  • Silver and copper salts are toxic to aquatic life: pour used solutions into the residue bottle, not down the sink.
  • Potassium nitrate is an oxidising agent. Wash hands after handling the soaked filter paper.

Part 1Do the experiment

Half-cells: 1.0 mol dm−3 solutions at 298 K
Equipment

Drag an item to where it goes, or tap it and then tap the place.

Method

  1. Clean the metal strips with emery paper.
  2. Put two half-cells on the bench.
  3. Join them with a salt bridge: filter paper soaked in saturated KNO3(aq).
  4. Place the high-resistance voltmeter and connect the leads.
  5. Get a positive reading and record Ecell.
  6. Repeat for at least 4 different cells.

2 Use these data. Fe3+/Fe2+ +0.77 V; I2/I− +0.54 V; Br2/Br− +1.09 V; Cu2+/Cu +0.34 V. Which reaction is thermodynamically feasible? (1)

A: E⦵cell = 0.77 − 0.54 = +0.23 V, positive so feasible. B, C and D all give negative E⦵cell.

3 Which change would increase the measured e.m.f. of the cell Zn(s)|Zn2+(aq)||Cu2+(aq)|Cu(s)? (1)

B: lower [Zn2+] moves Zn2+ + 2e− ⇌ Zn to the left, so E(Zn) becomes more negative and Ecell increases. Electrode size and bridge length do not change the e.m.f.
