Compare: giant covalent structures

In diamond and graphite every carbon atom is joined to others by strong covalent bonds in a giant network. There are no separate molecules. To melt them, many strong covalent bonds must break, so their melting points are very high (over 3500 °C).

Key points

  • Metal atoms lose electrons to form positive ions. Non-metal atoms gain electrons to form negative ions. The ions have a full outer shell (a noble gas electron configuration).
  • An ionic bond is the strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. Ionic compounds form giant lattices with high melting and boiling points.
  • Ionic compounds conduct electricity when molten or in aqueous solution because the ions can move. They do not conduct when solid because the ions are fixed in place.
  • A covalent bond is a shared pair of electrons, held by the electrostatic attraction between the shared pair and the nuclei of both atoms. A double bond is 2 shared pairs; a triple bond is 3.
  • Simple molecular substances have weak intermolecular forces between molecules, so they have low melting and boiling points. Melting and boiling do not break the covalent bonds. Melting and boiling points generally increase as relative molecular mass increases.
  • Covalent substances do not usually conduct electricity. Giant covalent structures have very high melting points because many strong covalent bonds must be broken.
