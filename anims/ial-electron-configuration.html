  1. The maximum number of electrons in a 3d sub-shell is .

    A d sub-shell has 5 orbitals and each orbital holds 2 electrons: 5 × 2 = 10.

  2. The electronic configuration of copper (Z = 29) is .

    Copper is an exception: a full 3d10 sub-shell is more stable, so one 4s electron goes into 3d.

  3. The Fe2+ ion (iron, Z = 26) has the configuration .

    Fe is [Ar] 3d6 4s2. When the ion forms, the two 4s electrons are lost first.

  4. Germanium is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p². It is in the .

    The highest-energy electrons are in a p sub-shell (4p), so germanium is in the p block.

Exam tip

  • Write every sub-shell with the number of electrons as a superscript: 1s2 2s2 2p6… You may use [Ar] for the first 18 electrons unless the question asks for the full configuration.
  • 4s fills before 3d, but 4s electrons are lost first when a transition metal forms an ion.
  • Learn the two exceptions: Cr is [Ar] 3d5 4s1 and Cu is [Ar] 3d10 4s1.
  • In box diagrams, put one arrow in each orbital before pairing (Hund's rule). Paired arrows must point in opposite directions.
