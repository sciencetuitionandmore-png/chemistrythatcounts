Key points

  • Particles must collide with at least the activation energy (Ea) to react. Ea is the minimum energy needed. Other collisions are unsuccessful: the particles just bounce off.
  • Concentration: more particles in the same volume, so more frequent collisions (more collisions per second).
  • Surface area: smaller pieces or powder expose more particles of the solid, so more frequent collisions.
  • Temperature: particles move faster, so more frequent collisions, and a greater proportion of particles have energy ≥ Ea, so a greater proportion of collisions are successful.
  • Catalyst: provides an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy, so more collisions are successful. The catalyst is not used up.
  • On a graph of product against time, the gradient is the rate. It is steepest at the start and levels off when a reactant is used up. The final amount of product depends on the amount of reactant, not on the rate.
