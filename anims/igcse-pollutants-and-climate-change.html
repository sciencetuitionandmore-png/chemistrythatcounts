Pollutants from burning fuels

Fuels such as petrol, diesel, coal and natural gas contain hydrocarbons. When they burn, they make gases that can harm people and the environment.

What's happening
Ways to reduce CO2: burn less fossil fuel; use renewable energy (wind, solar, hydroelectric); save energy; stop cutting down forests and plant more trees.

Key points

  • Complete combustion of a hydrocarbon (plenty of oxygen) makes carbon dioxide and water. With a limited supply of oxygen, incomplete combustion also makes carbon monoxide and carbon (soot).
  • Carbon monoxide is toxic. It is colourless and odourless. It combines with haemoglobin in red blood cells and reduces the ability of the blood to carry oxygen.
  • Fuels such as coal contain sulfur impurities. These burn to make sulfur dioxide: S + O2 → SO2.
  • In hot car engines, nitrogen and oxygen from the air react to make nitrogen oxides (NOx). The nitrogen does not come from the fuel.
  • SO2 and NOx dissolve in rain water to make acid rain. It damages trees, makes lakes acidic so fish die, and corrodes limestone buildings and metal structures.
  • Greenhouse gases (carbon dioxide, methane, water vapour) absorb heat given out by the Earth and re-emit some back. Burning fossil fuels and deforestation increase CO2, so more heat is kept in: global warming leads to climate change.
