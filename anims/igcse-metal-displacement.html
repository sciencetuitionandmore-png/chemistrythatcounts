What's happening

Key points

  • A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from a solution of its salt, or from its oxide.
  • If the metal added is less reactive than the metal in the compound, there is no reaction.
  • Observations: a coating of the displaced metal forms, the solution may change colour (for example blue copper(II) sulfate fades), and the temperature rises because the reaction is exothermic.
  • From displacement results the order is magnesium > zinc > iron > copper.
  • Displacement is a redox reaction. The more reactive metal is oxidised: it loses electrons (or gains oxygen). The less reactive metal's ion (or oxide) is reduced: it gains electrons (or loses oxygen).
  • Sulfate ions do not change. They are spectator ions, so they are left out of the ionic equation.
