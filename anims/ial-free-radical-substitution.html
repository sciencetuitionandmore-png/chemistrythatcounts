Part 3Check it

  1. The reaction of methane with chlorine needs .

    UV light provides the energy to break the Cl–Cl bond in the initiation step.

  2. In the initiation step the Cl–Cl bond breaks by .

    Homolytic fission: each Cl atom takes one electron from the bond pair, forming two radicals.

  3. Which equation is a termination step? .

    In termination two radicals join to make a molecule, so no radicals are left.

  4. The reaction gives a mixture of products because .

    Further substitution makes CH2Cl2, CHCl3 and CCl4. Termination also makes some ethane.

Exam tip

  • Always give the condition: UV light (or sunlight). Heat alone is not the answer the mark scheme wants.
  • Put the radical dot on the correct atom: •CH3 has the dot on the carbon, not on a hydrogen.
  • Propagation steps have one radical on each side. Termination steps have two radicals on the left and none on the right.
  • Fish-hook (half-headed) arrows show one electron moving. Full arrowheads would be wrong here.
