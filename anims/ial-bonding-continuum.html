  1. The cation with the greatest polarising power is .

    Al3+ is the smallest ion here and has the highest charge, so it has the highest charge density.

  2. The anion that is most easily polarised is .

    I− is the largest. Its outer electrons are far from the nucleus and well shielded, so its electron cloud is easily distorted.

  3. Aluminium chloride has a lot of covalent character because .

    The high charge density of Al3+ polarises Cl−, pulling electron density into the space between the ions.

  4. Electronegativity values: H 2.1, C 2.5, Cl 3.0, O 3.5, F 4.0. The most polar bond is .

    The differences are C–H 0.4, C–Cl 0.5, O–H 1.4 and H–F 1.9. The biggest difference gives the most polar bond.

Exam tip

  • Polarising power depends on charge density: a small cation with a high charge (such as Al3+) polarises most.
  • Say that the cation distorts the electron cloud of the anion. The anion is polarised, not the cation.
  • Large anions such as I− are most polarisable: their outer electrons are far from the nucleus and well shielded.
  • Put δ− on the more electronegative atom and δ+ on the other. A bond between two identical atoms is non-polar.
