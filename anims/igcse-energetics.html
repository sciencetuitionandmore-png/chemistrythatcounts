Energy changes in reactions

Some reactions give out heat energy and some take in heat energy. We find out which by measuring the temperature change. Then we can calculate the energy change.

Key points

  • Exothermic: heat energy is given out to the surroundings. The temperature of the surroundings rises. ΔH is negative. Examples: combustion, neutralisation, displacement, respiration.
  • Endothermic: heat energy is taken in from the surroundings. The temperature falls. ΔH is positive. Examples: thermal decomposition, photosynthesis, dissolving some salts (e.g. ammonium nitrate).
  • In solution: use a polystyrene cup with a lid. Measure the volumes, record the starting temperature, mix and stir, then record the highest (or lowest) temperature. For combustion: heat water in a copper can with a spirit burner and weigh the burner before and after.
  • Q = m × c × ΔT. Q is in J, m is the mass of the solution or water in g (1 cm³ of solution has a mass of 1 g), c = 4.2 J/g/°C, ΔT is in °C.
  • ΔH = −Q ÷ n, with Q in kJ and n in mol, so ΔH is in kJ/mol. Temperature rose → ΔH negative. Temperature fell → ΔH positive.
  • Heat losses to the surroundings make ΔT too small, so the measured ΔH is less exothermic (or less endothermic) than the data book value.
