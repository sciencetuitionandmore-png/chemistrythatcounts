Part 3Check it

  1. An electrophile is .

    An electrophile accepts a pair of electrons, here from the π bond of the alkene.

  2. In the first step, the H–Br bond breaks by .

    Both electrons of the H–Br bond go to Br, making Br−: heterolytic fission.

  3. The major product of propene with HBr is .

    The secondary carbocation has two electron-releasing alkyl groups, so it is more stable and forms more often.

  4. When an alkene is shaken with bromine water, the colour changes from .

    Bromine adds across the C=C bond, so the orange colour disappears. Say "colourless", not "clear".

Exam tip

  • The first curly arrow starts from the middle of the C=C double bond and ends on the δ+ H (or δ+ Br). Show the δ+ and δ− charges.
  • The second arrow starts from the H–Br bond and goes to the Br atom.
  • Draw Br− with a lone pair, and start the third arrow from that lone pair, ending on the C+.
  • Bromine water turns from orange to colourless. "Clear" does not score the mark.
