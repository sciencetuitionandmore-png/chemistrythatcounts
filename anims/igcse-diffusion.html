Key points

  • Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration. It is caused by the random motion of the particles.
  • Diffusion is faster in gases than in liquids. Gas particles move faster and are far apart. In a liquid the particles are close together and collide very often.
  • Diffusion is faster at a higher temperature, because the particles have more kinetic energy and move faster.
  • Lighter particles diffuse faster. NH3 (Mr 17) moves faster than HCl (Mr 36.5), so the white ring of ammonium chloride forms nearer the hydrochloric acid end.
  • Gas particles collide with air particles, so diffusion in air is slow. In a vacuum there is nothing to collide with, so the gas spreads almost instantly.
  • Potassium manganate(VII) solution is still coloured after many dilutions. This shows there are a very large number of very small particles.
