Key points

  • Cracking breaks long-chain alkanes into shorter-chain alkanes and alkenes.
  • It is needed because crude oil gives more long-chain fractions (fuel oil, bitumen) than we need, and not enough short-chain fractions such as petrol (gasoline). Alkenes are used to make polymers.
  • Industrial cracking uses a catalyst of silica or alumina at about 600–700 °C.
  • Alkanes have the general formula CnH2n+2 and are saturated (C–C single bonds only). Alkenes have the general formula CnH2n and are unsaturated (they contain a C=C double bond).
  • A cracking equation must balance: the numbers of C and H atoms are the same on both sides, e.g. C10H22 → C8H18 + C2H4.
  • Bromine water turns from orange to colourless with an alkene (bromine adds across the C=C). With an alkane it stays orange.
