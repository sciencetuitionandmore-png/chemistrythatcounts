IGCSE Chemistry · Organic chemistry: crude oil

Fractional distillation of crude oil

Crude oil is a mixture of hydrocarbons (compounds of hydrogen and carbon only). Most of them are alkanes. Fractional distillation separates crude oil into fractions: groups of hydrocarbons with similar boiling points.

Key points

  • Crude oil is a mixture of hydrocarbons. Most of them are alkanes. A hydrocarbon is a compound of hydrogen and carbon only.
  • Crude oil is heated until most of it vaporises. The vapour enters near the bottom of the fractionating column.
  • The column is hot at the bottom and cool at the top. Each hydrocarbon condenses at the level where the temperature is below its boiling point, and is collected there.
  • Fractions from bottom to top: bitumen, fuel oil, diesel, kerosene, gasoline, refinery gases. Uses: roads and roofs; ships and power stations; diesel engines; aircraft fuel; car petrol; bottled gas for heating and cooking.
  • From top to bottom: chain length increases, boiling point increases, viscosity increases, colour gets darker, and fractions are harder to ignite.
  • Longer molecules have stronger intermolecular forces, so more energy is needed to overcome them. Boiling breaks intermolecular forces, not covalent bonds.
