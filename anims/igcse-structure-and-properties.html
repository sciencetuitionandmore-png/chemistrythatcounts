Key points

  • Giant ionic lattices have many strong electrostatic attractions between oppositely charged ions in all directions. Lots of energy is needed to overcome them, so melting and boiling points are high.
  • Ionic compounds do not conduct when solid (ions are fixed). They conduct when molten or in aqueous solution because the ions are free to move.
  • Simple molecular substances have strong covalent bonds inside molecules but weak intermolecular forces between molecules. Melting and boiling overcome the intermolecular forces. The covalent bonds do not break.
  • Melting and boiling points of simple molecular substances generally increase as relative molecular mass increases. They do not conduct: there are no ions or delocalised electrons.
  • Giant covalent structures have very high melting points: many strong covalent bonds must break. Diamond: each C bonded to 4 others, very hard, does not conduct. Graphite: each C bonded to 3 others in layers with weak forces between them, so layers slide (soft, lubricant). One delocalised electron per atom moves along the layers, so graphite conducts.
