Part 3Check it

  1. The shape of an ammonia molecule, NH3, is .

    N has 3 bonding pairs and 1 lone pair. Four pairs point to the corners of a tetrahedron, but only 3 have atoms: pyramidal.

  2. The H–O–H bond angle in water is .

    Two lone pairs repel more than bonding pairs, so the angle closes from 109.5° to 104.5°.

  3. The bond angle in NH3 is smaller than in CH4 because .

    The lone pair is closer to the nucleus and repels the bonding pairs more, pushing the N–H bonds together.

  4. PCl5 has five bonding pairs around P. Its bond angles are .

    Trigonal bipyramidal: 120° between the three equatorial bonds and 90° between axial and equatorial bonds.

Exam tip

  • Say that electron pairs repel and get as far apart as possible. Do not say that atoms or bonds repel.
  • Always state the number of bonding pairs and lone pairs before you name the shape.
  • Lone pairs repel more than bonding pairs, so each lone pair closes the bond angle by about 2.5°.
  • For ions, adjust the electrons for the charge: NH4+ has 4 bonding pairs and no lone pairs, so it is tetrahedral, 109.5°.
  • In a dot-and-cross diagram of an ion, draw square brackets round the whole ion and write the charge outside. A dative bond has both electrons from the same atom.
