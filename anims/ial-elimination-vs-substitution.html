Exam tip

  • Give conditions in full: substitution — KOH(aq), warm (or heat under reflux); elimination — KOH dissolved in ethanol, heat under reflux.
  • E2 needs three curly arrows: lone pair on O → H on the carbon next to the C–Br carbon; C–H bond → between the two carbons; C–Br bond → Br.
  • Say what OH− does: in substitution it acts as a nucleophile (attacks δ+ C); in elimination it acts as a base (removes H+).
  • For an unsymmetrical halogenoalkane give every alkene, including the E and Z isomers of but-2-ene.

Key fact: water as solvent favours substitution (alcohol); ethanol as solvent and a higher temperature favour elimination (alkene + water + halide ion).
